The two update rules

ordinary ⨯ ordinary: a, b → (a+b)/2, (a+b)/2
conscious ⨯ ordinary: v → v/2,   v_c → min(1, 2·v_c)

Averaging conserves the pair's sum exactly — it only spreads violence around, it never removes any. Only contact with the conscious body deletes anything: half of the struck body's value simply vanishes from the system. The conscious body's own doubling is a separate, independent bookkeeping line, not a transfer — it is not "absorbing" what it removes, it's catalyzing the loss while barely changing itself. That asymmetry is the whole mechanism.

The decay law for the whole room

Let S = total violence, N = body count. Any random collision has probability 2/N of touching the conscious body; when it does, the partner (≈ the population mean m) loses m/2. Averaging collisions leave S untouched. So per collision:

E[ΔS] = (2/N)·(−m/2) ≈ −S/N²

which integrates to a clean exponential — no approximation needed beyond mean-field mixing:

S(t) ≈ S₀ · e^(−t/N²)

Verifying "it won't need to be worried about"

Reaching 1% of the starting total takes t₉₉ = N²·ln(100) collisions. The conscious body is expected to be part of only 2/N of them:

n_c = 2N·ln(100) ≈ 9.2N doublings
