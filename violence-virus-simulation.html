Violence Virus Threshold

One body starts at 10⁻²⁰⁰ percent violence. Every collision it takes part in halves the other body's violence and doubles its own — and any body whose violence is driven below the conversion threshold flips permanently into the same role, halving whoever it touches from then on. Two ordinary bodies still just average on contact. Watch calm spread the way violence usually does.

The update rules

ordinary ⨯ ordinary: a, b → (a+b)/2, (a+b)/2
conscious ⨯ ordinary: v → v/2,   v_role → min(1, 2·v_role)
if v ≤ T after that hit: body flips to conscious, permanently

Accounting rule: once a body is flagged conscious, its own violence value stops counting toward the room's total. It still exists — click it in the inspector to see its exact number, which can drift back up from repeated doubling as it keeps disarming others — but a conscious body's residual value is treated as no longer live in the system, the way a recovered case stops counting as infectious even though the virus's genetic material might still be technically present. Without this, the total could visibly tick back up near the end of a run, when most collisions are between two already-conscious bodies with occasionally-inflated values — an artifact of what's being counted, not a real resurgence of violence. The simulation auto-pauses and the chart auto-shrinks to the full run the instant every body converts, since at that point the (correctly accounted) total is exactly zero and there's nothing left to simulate.

"Total system violence" is a plain sum, not an average — ten bodies sitting at 18% each read as 180.0, not 18.0%. With N bodies starting near 100% each, expect the starting number in the thousands; the chart's total-violence line still plots it as a fraction of that starting sum, purely so it fits the same 0–100% axis as the other two lines.

Averaging conserves the pair's sum — it only spreads violence around, never removes any. Only a conscious-role hit deletes anything, and it also grows the population that can delete things: every body it drives below the threshold T becomes a second source of the same effect. That's a bootstrap cascade (the same shape as bootstrap percolation / the Watts threshold model of social contagion), not a fixed sink — the "infection" here is calm, and it compounds.

Why one seed is enough for all of them

Fix any body still above T. Each time it collides with a conscious-flagged body, its value is exactly halved — a geometric decay with no floor besides 0. However small T is, that body crosses it after a finite number of such hits:

hits needed ≈ log₂(v / T)

In a well-mixed chamber every pair collides again and again over time, so the probability any one body is never hit by a conscious body, forever, is zero. Conclusion: for any fixed T > 0 with no ambient term, full conversion — every body conscious, total violence → 0 — isn't just likely, it's the guaranteed long-run outcome.

What the ambient slider breaks

The ambient term adds a continuous drift dv/dt = rate to every body, independent of collisions — a background field, not an interaction. That guarantee above assumed nothing was fighting the cascade. A positive rate is exactly that fight: it can drag a body back over T between hits, faster than the cascade can push it down. There's a real tug-of-war here between a discrete, collision-driven cooling process and a continuous, time-driven heating one — set the rate negative and you're adding a second calming channel on top of the cascade; push it toward +10 and you're watching the same "runs away past a critical value" behavior from the very first version of this model, just driven by a clock instead of a doubling.

Verifying the cascade actually accelerates

With C conscious bodies out of N, a random collision has probability ≈ 2C(N−C)/[N(N−1)/2] of pairing a conscious body with an ordinary one — that probability, and so the expected conversion rate, rises as C grows, then falls back off as ordinary bodies run out. That's a classic S-curve: slow start, fast middle, saturating tail — the same shape as an epidemic curve, just running toward zero violence instead of away from it. At the current slider values:

Bodies are labeled with their live violence percentage. Fill color runs a log-scaled spectrum from bright green at (or below) the conversion threshold up to a body's "hot" end — dark red for a body not yet conscious, gold for one that is: a conscious body's own value still climbs back up from repeated doubling, but it never reads as red again, because the color is meant to say what the body is doing, not just what number it's holding. The gold ring marks any body currently flagged conscious as a secondary cue at any zoom level. A conscious body whose own value has climbed back to essentially 100% gets a faint yellow halo on top of that — a body can be holding a full charge of violence internally and still, by role, be the one halving whoever it touches; the halo is there so that fact reads at a glance instead of getting lost in "it's basically red again." Chamber is compressed and bodies are small (radius halved from an earlier version) to fit up to 200 on screen; every ball carries a deliberately tiny live percentage label — click one to read its exact value in the inspector above the chart. Ambient drift only touches bodies not yet flagged conscious, so a body's crossing into consciousness is a real, permanent immunity to the background field, not just a color that happens to sit near green.
