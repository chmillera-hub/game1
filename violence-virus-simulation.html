The update rules

non-conscious ⨯ non-conscious: transfer = clamp((b−a)/2, ±cap); a → a+transfer, b → b−transfer
conscious ⨯ non-conscious: v → max(0, v/2 − bonus),   v_role stays 0 (2·0 = 0)
bonus = max(0, radiation %/s) × (miracle power / 100), in percentage points
if v ≤ T after that hit: v is reset to exactly 0, body flips to conscious, permanently

The simplification: a body doesn't drift down to near zero and then keep existing at some tiny residual — the instant it crosses T, its value is reset to exactly 0. Every conscious body's own violence is therefore always precisely 0, forever: the seed starts there, and every later conversion lands there too. Doubling a body that's exactly 0 leaves it at exactly 0 (0·2=0), so there's no drift, no residual number to worry about, and no separate "has it climbed back up" question to track. Once a body converts it stops counting toward the room's total for the simplest possible reason: its own value really is 0, not merely excluded by convention. Consequently the total can only reach exactly 0 once every body has converted — it is mathematically impossible for one to happen without the other.

"Total system violence" is a plain sum, not an average — ten bodies sitting at 18% each read as 180.0, not 18.0%. With N bodies starting near 100% each, expect the starting number in the thousands; the chart's total-violence line still plots the live, instantaneous sum as a fraction of that starting sum, purely so it fits the same 0–100% axis as the other two lines. The stat card and sticky bar, however, don't show that live number at all — they show the lowest this sum has ever been so far in the run, with the elapsed time that low was hit in parentheses right after it, since total violence isn't monotonic (background violence radiation keeps adding to it between hits, and a positive setting can push it back up faster than collisions bring it down) and the running minimum is the more useful "how much progress has this run banked" number to glance at.

Averaging conserves the pair's sum — it only spreads violence around, never removes any — and the "violence exchange cap" slider only throttles how much of that spread one hit can deliver: at its max (50, the largest possible half-gap between two bodies) it never binds, so nothing changes from the plain average. Dial it down and a single collision can only move a pair partway to their average, capped at that many percentage points; the two still keep colliding, so they keep closing the rest of the gap over several hits instead of one. This exists to protect progress a divine ball has already made on a body: without a cap, a body a divine ball has patiently halved down near threshold T (say to 1%) gets yanked straight back up to ~50% the instant it collides with one ordinary 100% body — one plain-average hit erases many divine hits' worth of work. With the cap on, that same collision only pulls the low body up by the capped amount (26% at cap=25, not 50%), so the divine ball's progress survives ordinary contact and it can keep working the body down toward T across repeated divine hits instead of racing an unbounded reset. Only a conscious-role hit deletes anything, and it also grows the population that can delete things: every body it drives below the threshold T becomes a second source of the same effect. That's a bootstrap cascade (the same shape as bootstrap percolation / the Watts threshold model of social contagion), not a fixed sink — the "infection" here is calm, and it compounds.

Why a single seed is already enough for all of them

Fix any body still above T. Each time it collides with a conscious-flagged body, its value is exactly halved — a geometric decay with no floor besides 0. However small T is, that body crosses it after a finite number of such hits:

hits needed ≈ log₂(v / T)

In a well-mixed chamber every pair collides again and again over time, so the probability any one body is never hit by a conscious body, forever, is zero. Conclusion: for any fixed T > 0 with no background violence radiation, full conversion — every body conscious, total violence → 0 — isn't just likely, it's the guaranteed long-run outcome.

What background violence radiation breaks

This slider models the ambient, everywhere-at-once violence of a world that keeps generating friction on its own — not a person-to-person interaction, but a background condition every still-non-conscious body is bathed in continuously, raising its violence by a fixed amount every second whether or not it's currently colliding with anything. It's a rising tide by design, from 0 (off) up to +10%/s: it actively works against the cascade, dragging a body's violence back up over T between hits, faster than conversions can push it down. There's a real tug-of-war between a discrete, collision-driven cooling process and this continuous, clock-driven heating one — push it toward +10 and you're watching the same "runs away past a critical value" behavior from the very first version of this model, just driven by a clock instead of a doubling. (There's no negative/"calming radiation" option — a contact-free channel straight to zero would trivially and near-instantly convert every body on its own, without any of the collision dynamics this simulation is actually about.)

Miracle power

Direct contact already halves a body; miracle power adds a flat bonus on top of that halving, sized as a fraction of whatever the radiation slider currently reads (never a fraction of the body's own violence). At radiation=10%/s and miracle=100%, a body at 100% goes to 50% from the halving, then to 40% from the bonus — one collision, two separate deductions. At the default miracle=0% this term is always exactly 0 and contact is plain halving, same as before this control existed. Raising it doesn't change the average outcome much on its own; it raises the odds of a body getting unlucky in one specific way — repeated direct contact with the same conscious body in a short span, each hit chipping off extra — crossing threshold T sooner, or even in a single hit, when plain halving alone wouldn't have gotten there yet. That's why the Chamber's live tag counts conscious hits (direct conscious-vs-non-conscious collisions) rather than every collision in the room: multiply that count by the current bonus per hit and you get the "Miracle violence removed" stat below directly — hits × bonus, no need to do the arithmetic by hand.

Verifying the cascade actually accelerates

With C conscious bodies out of N, a random collision has probability ≈ 2C(N−C)/[N(N−1)/2] of pairing a conscious body with a non-conscious one — that probability, and so the expected conversion rate, rises as C grows, then falls back off as non-conscious bodies run out. That's a classic S-curve: slow start, fast middle, saturating tail — the same shape as an epidemic curve, just running toward zero violence instead of away from it. At the current slider values:

A seed body's own number never moves at all — it's exactly 0 from the first frame to the last. It's the count of conscious bodies, not any one body's value, that does the work of erasing the room's violence — starting with more seeds only raises that count sooner.

Color is the only at-a-glance signal now — no on-ball text, and no gradient either: a non-conscious body is one of three fixed colors by band, not a ramp — red at 51% or above, green from 11% to 50%, yellow at 10% or below. Yellow exists specifically to flag "getting close to the conversion threshold" without implying it's already converted, since a body that's crossed below 10% is usually only a hit or two away. A conscious body is always exactly 0% violence, so it always gets the same fixed gold fill and gold ring — a brighter, cooler yellow (hue 58) was picked deliberately far from that warmer, darker gold (hue 38) so the two never get confused even at very small ball sizes. (The gold ring's faint halo turns off above 1,000 bodies, purely so thousands of per-ball gradients don't tank the frame rate — the flat gold fill stays either way.) Click any ball to read its exact value in the inspector above the chart. Bodies range from 10 up to 10,000, in steps of 10 below 100 and steps of 100 from 100 up, so the slider always lands on a round number instead of an arbitrary one; the chamber and ball radius scale together to keep a reasonable, visible size and packing density at any count, and past 300 bodies collision detection switches from checking every pair to a spatial grid (only nearby bodies are checked against each other) so the frame rate doesn't collapse at high counts. Background violence radiation only touches bodies not yet flagged conscious, so a body's crossing into consciousness is a real, permanent immunity to that rising background hazard. The bar under the chamber is a conversion replay, not a full-run scrubber: it only ever holds the first 20 conscious-ball formations, each captured as its own clip spanning 30 simulated seconds immediately before that ball crossed threshold, the crossing moment itself, and 10 simulated seconds immediately after -- long enough after to see whether the same divine ball (or another) goes on to hit it, or others nearby, again. Every physics step is captured individually rather than sampled over real time, so dragging within one clip shows the exact collision-by-collision sequence (at very high body counts, where a full-precision 40-second clip would use too much memory, capture spaces itself out just enough to stay within a fixed budget -- still every step at typical body counts). Everything else in the run -- the long stretches between conversions -- is simply never captured, so the bar has nothing to show there; drag past the end of one clip and you land on the start of the next. Dragging pauses the sim and repaints the chamber and headline stats as they were at that exact moment, and dragging back to the right end (or pressing Run) returns to live.
