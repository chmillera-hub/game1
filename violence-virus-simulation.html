Why it doesn't explode

Every collision applies v → 2v — pure contagion, unbounded. The conscious body also gets doubled on contact; there is no exemption. What saves it is a fixed subtraction k applied immediately after:

vn+1 = max(0, 2·vn − k)

This affine map has one fixed point, found by setting vn+1=vn=v*:

v* = 2v* − k ⟶ v* = k

It's unstable — slope 2 at the fixed point. Below v*, each iteration nets a decrease (2v−k < v exactly when v<k), and the floor at 0 turns "decrease" into "absorbed at zero forever." Above v*, each iteration nets an increase that compounds — divergence. v*=k is the separatrix: a critical mass, an epidemic's R₀=1, a nuclear pile's k-effective=1. Same shape, different noun.

Closed form & why 10⁻¹⁴ survives

vn = 2n(v₀ − k) + k

Starting at v₀=10⁻¹⁴ with k=0.01, deviation (v₀−k)≈−0.01 gets doubled each hit, but it takes n≈log₂(0.01/10⁻¹⁴)≈40 doublings just to erode the gap — and the floor clips it to exactly 0 the first time the raw value would go negative, which happens on the very first hit. So the "conscious" body isn't slowly winning a race against infinity; the subtraction makes 0 an absorbing state before growth ever gets a foothold.
