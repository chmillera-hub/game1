The update rules

non-conscious ⨯ non-conscious: transfer = clamp((b−a)/2, ±cap); a → a+transfer, b → b−transfer
conscious ⨯ non-conscious: v → max(0, v/2 − bonus),   v_role stays 0 (2·0 = 0)
bonus = max(0, radiation %/s) × (miracle power / 100), in percentage points
if v ≤ T after that hit: v is reset to exactly 0, body flips to conscious, permanently (unless corrupted by a devil ball -- see below)

The simplification: a body doesn't drift down to near zero and then keep existing at some tiny residual — the instant it crosses T, its value is reset to exactly 0. Every conscious body's own violence is therefore always precisely 0, forever: the seed starts there, and every later conversion lands there too. Doubling a body that's exactly 0 leaves it at exactly 0 (0·2=0), so there's no drift, no residual number to worry about, and no separate "has it climbed back up" question to track. Once a body converts it stops counting toward the room's total for the simplest possible reason: its own value really is 0, not merely excluded by convention. Consequently the total can only reach exactly 0 once every body has converted — it is mathematically impossible for one to happen without the other. (This permanence has exactly one exception: with devil balls in play, repeated devil hits can drive a divine ball's v back up to exactly 1, at which point it corrupts back into a devil ball itself — see "Devil balls" below. With Devil balls = 0, the default, none of this applies and conversion really is permanent.)

"Total system violence" is a plain sum, not an average — ten bodies sitting at 18% each read as 180.0, not 18.0%. With N bodies starting near 100% each, expect the starting number in the thousands; the chart's total-violence line still plots the live, instantaneous sum as a fraction of that starting sum, purely so it fits the same 0–100% axis as the other two lines. The stat card and sticky bar, however, don't show that live number at all — they show the lowest this sum has ever been so far in the run, with the elapsed time that low was hit in parentheses right after it, since total violence isn't monotonic (background violence radiation keeps adding to it between hits, and a positive setting can push it back up faster than collisions bring it down) and the running minimum is the more useful "how much progress has this run banked" number to glance at.

Averaging conserves the pair's sum — it only spreads violence around, never removes any — and the "violence exchange cap" slider throttles how much of that spread one hit can deliver, at every setting, with no "uncapped" option anywhere on it. Between two ordinary bodies, the cap's max (50) happens to equal the largest possible half-gap between them, so it never binds any tighter than plain averaging already would on its own — that's a coincidence of the math (100% apart, split in half, is 50), not a special uncapped mode. Dial it down and a single collision can only move a pair partway to their average, capped at that many percentage points; the two still keep colliding, so they keep closing the rest of the gap over several hits instead of one. This exists to protect progress a divine ball has already made on a body: without a cap, a body a divine ball has patiently halved down near threshold T (say to 1%) gets yanked straight back up to ~50% the instant it collides with one ordinary 100% body — one plain-average hit erases many divine hits' worth of work. With the cap on, that same collision only pulls the low body up by the capped amount (26% at cap=25, not 50%), so the divine ball's progress survives ordinary contact and it can keep working the body down toward T across repeated divine hits instead of racing an unbounded reset. Devil-ball donation (see below) uses this same slider but the cap genuinely binds there even at 50, since a devil can hold up to 100% to give away — a 100%-violence devil touching a 1% body raises it to at most 51% at cap=50, never anywhere close to 100% in one hit. Only a conscious-role hit deletes anything, and it also grows the population that can delete things: every body it drives below the threshold T becomes a second source of the same effect. That's a bootstrap cascade (the same shape as bootstrap percolation / the Watts threshold model of social contagion), not a fixed sink — the "infection" here is calm, and it compounds.

Why a single seed is already enough for all of them

Fix any body still above T. Each time it collides with a conscious-flagged body, its value is exactly halved — a geometric decay with no floor besides 0. However small T is, that body crosses it after a finite number of such hits:

hits needed ≈ log₂(v / T)

In a well-mixed chamber every pair collides again and again over time, so the probability any one body is never hit by a conscious body, forever, is zero. Conclusion: for any fixed T > 0 with no background violence radiation and no devil balls, full conversion — every body conscious, total violence → 0 — isn't just likely, it's the guaranteed long-run outcome. Two sliders can each break that guarantee in their own way, one on by default (radiation) and one off by default (devil balls) — read on for what each one does.

What background violence radiation breaks

This slider models the ambient, everywhere-at-once violence of a world that keeps generating friction on its own — not a person-to-person interaction, but a background condition every still-non-conscious body is bathed in continuously, raising its violence by a fixed amount every second whether or not it's currently colliding with anything. It's a rising tide by design, from +0.001%/s up to +10%/s: it actively works against the cascade, dragging a body's violence back up over T between hits, faster than conversions can push it down. There's a real tug-of-war between a discrete, collision-driven cooling process and this continuous, clock-driven heating one — push it toward +10 and you're watching the same "runs away past a critical value" behavior from the very first version of this model, just driven by a clock instead of a doubling. (There's no negative/"calming radiation" option, and no exact-zero option either — a contact-free channel straight to zero would trivially and near-instantly convert every body on its own with the divine side guaranteed to win, bypassing the collision dynamics this simulation is actually about; a real world always has some baseline source of friction above zero — comets, asteroids, natural disasters — so the slider's floor reflects that a truly violence-free background isn't a realistic setting to leave on, even if it's a fine idealization to reason about mathematically.)

Miracle power

Direct contact already halves a body; miracle power adds a flat bonus on top of that halving, sized as a fraction of whatever the radiation slider currently reads (never a fraction of the body's own violence). At radiation=10%/s and miracle=100%, a body at 100% goes to 50% from the halving, then to 40% from the bonus — one collision, two separate deductions. At the default miracle=0% this term is always exactly 0 and contact is plain halving, same as before this control existed. Raising it doesn't change the average outcome much on its own; it raises the odds of a body getting unlucky in one specific way — repeated direct contact with the same conscious body in a short span, each hit chipping off extra — crossing threshold T sooner, or even in a single hit, when plain halving alone wouldn't have gotten there yet. That's why the Chamber's live tag counts conscious hits (direct conscious-vs-non-conscious collisions) rather than every collision in the room: multiply that count by the current bonus per hit and you get the "Miracle violence removed" stat below directly — hits × bonus, no need to do the arithmetic by hand.

Devil balls

Off by default (0 starting devils). A devil ball's defining property, permanent for as long as it stays a devil: it can never lose violence to another violent body, ever, of any kind — the only thing that ever reduces a devil ball's own v is a direct hit from a divine ball, and even that only starts working once every normal violent body (non-seed, non-devil) has converted (the "minions" have to be cleared first). Touching any other still-violent body — an ordinary minion, or another devil — never averages with it; instead the side with MORE violence donates a flat dose onto the side with less, capped at both the exchange-cap slider AND the donor's own current value (it can't give away more violence than it currently holds), while the donor's own v never drops. So a devil sitting at exactly 100% (its value before it's ever been hit by anything) donates the full exchange-cap amount every touch, an effectively infinite fountain; a devil that's since been knocked down to, say, 10% by divine hits can still only ever donate up to that 10% per touch, never more, and still never loses any of it doing so. Contact with a divine ball specifically is the other half of the asymmetry: instead of donating, the devil ball adds a flat percentage (the "devil damage" slider) straight onto that divine ball's violence, every hit — enough repeated hits and the divine ball's v climbs all the way back to exactly 100%, at which point it corrupts: it stops being conscious and becomes a devil ball itself, undoing one of the run's conversions; if every single conscious body eventually gets corrupted this way, the devils have won outright and the run ends (see the milestone banner). Once every normal violent body has converted, a devil ball becomes vulnerable to divine halving for the first time and can itself eventually convert once its v drops to threshold T — but it still never loses anything to another devil, even after that point; only a divine hit ever pulls a devil's own number down. The intended shape: normal violent bodies are the "minions" that have to be cleared first, since they're the only thing standing between the room and full consciousness while devils stonewall (and actively re-arm) every violent body around them; the devils are the "boss fight" that only becomes winnable once the minions are gone, and even then only divine contact -- never devil infighting -- ever chips away at one.

Verifying the cascade actually accelerates

With C conscious bodies out of N, a random collision has probability ≈ 2C(N−C)/[N(N−1)/2] of pairing a conscious body with a non-conscious one — that probability, and so the expected conversion rate, rises as C grows, then falls back off as non-conscious bodies run out. That's a classic S-curve: slow start, fast middle, saturating tail — the same shape as an epidemic curve, just running toward zero violence instead of away from it. At the current slider values:

A seed body's own number never moves at all — it's exactly 0 from the first frame to the last. It's the count of conscious bodies, not any one body's value, that does the work of erasing the room's violence — starting with more seeds only raises that count sooner.

Color is the only at-a-glance signal now — no on-ball text, and no gradient either: a non-conscious body is one of three fixed colors by band, not a ramp — red at 51% or above, green from 11% to 50%, yellow at 10% or below. Yellow exists specifically to flag "getting close to the conversion threshold" without implying it's already converted, since a body that's crossed below 10% is usually only a hit or two away. A conscious body is always exactly 0% violence, so it always gets the same fixed gold fill and gold ring — a brighter, cooler yellow (hue 58) was picked deliberately far from that warmer, darker gold (hue 38) so the two never get confused even at very small ball sizes. (The gold ring's faint halo turns off above 1,000 bodies, purely so thousands of per-ball gradients don't tank the frame rate — the flat gold fill stays either way.) Click any ball to read its exact value in the inspector above the chart. Bodies range from 10 up to 10,000, in steps of 10 below 100 and steps of 100 from 100 up, so the slider always lands on a round number instead of an arbitrary one; the chamber and ball radius scale together to keep a reasonable, visible size and packing density at any count, and past 300 bodies collision detection switches from checking every pair to a spatial grid (only nearby bodies are checked against each other) so the frame rate doesn't collapse at high counts. Background violence radiation only touches bodies not yet flagged conscious, so a body's crossing into consciousness is a real immunity to that rising background hazard -- permanent, unless devil balls are in play and later corrupt it back (see the "Devil balls" section in the explainer above, and the dark-red-aura chip in the legend). The bar under the chamber is a conversion replay, not a full-run scrubber: it only ever holds the first 20 conscious-ball formations, each captured as its own clip spanning 15 simulated seconds immediately before that ball crossed threshold, the crossing moment itself, and 5 simulated seconds immediately after -- long enough after to see whether the same divine ball (or another) goes on to hit it, or others nearby, again. Every physics step is captured individually rather than sampled over real time (at very high body counts, where a full-precision 20-second clip would use too much memory, capture spaces itself out just enough to stay within a fixed budget -- still every step at typical body counts). Whichever body that clip is about gets an extra purple glow and ring throughout the entire clip, on top of whatever color it's already showing (red, green, or yellow beforehand, gold after) -- so it stays trackable as the one specific ball being followed, not lost in a crowd of same-colored bodies, and you can watch it get hit again within the same clip if it does. That same body is also auto-selected in the inspector for the whole clip, so its exact violence number updates live as the clip plays or you scrub through it, without having to click it yourself -- click any other ball in the clip to inspect that one instead, until the next clip is picked. Everything else in the run -- the long stretches between conversions -- is simply never captured. Browsing is Prev/Next-driven, not one giant bar for every clip stitched together: Prev/Next picks which conversion you're looking at (label shows which one and its crossing value), and the slider only ever spans that one clip's own frames, so dragging it stays fine-grained instead of each pixel of mouse movement skipping over dozens of frames the way one shared 20-clip-long bar would. The ◀/▶ buttons step exactly one frame at a time for total precision, and "Play clip" auto-advances through the whole thing at a fixed slow pace if you'd rather just watch it than drag anything. Selecting a clip pauses the sim and repaints the chamber and headline stats at that exact frame; "Back to live" (or pressing Run) returns to the live view.
