# Audio: score, sound effects, mix

Everything you hear apart from the voices is generated by code in this repo: an original orchestral score
rendered with the FluidR3 GM soundfont, about 30 synthesized cartoon SFX plus two ambience beds, and a
re-recording mix with per-character voice processing. All audio is 44.1 kHz stereo.

```
python3 audio/tts.py && python3 audio/vocals.py   # voices (Kokoro TTS; separate scripts)
python3 tools/build_timeline.py                   # data/timeline.json (absolute beat times + ^/~ word marks)
tools/build_audio.sh                              # = the four steps below
  python3 audio/sfx.py                            #   SFX library -> audio/sfx/*.wav + meta.json (once, ~12 s)
  python3 tools/collect_sfx.py                    #   -> data/sfx_events.json
  python3 audio/music.py                          #   -> data/music_plan.json (auto), out/music.wav, out/stems/music_*.wav
  python3 audio/mix.py                            #   -> out/mix.wav   (what tools/encode.sh muxes)
```
Run times on this machine: score about 70 s for 5 minutes of film, mix about 50 s.
**Re-run collect_sfx, music and mix every time the timeline changes**, for example after TTS or after a
screenplay edit. Accents, hits and SFX are placed by absolute time.

Before TTS exists, `python3 tools/build_timeline.py --estimate` builds a timeline from word counts. The
whole chain still runs: missing voice files are treated as silence, and word-mark times are estimated
from each word's position in its line.

## Files

| file | what |
|---|---|
| `audio/audiolib.py` | DSP toolkit (load/save, EQ, synthetic stereo reverb, compressor, side-chain `duck`, BS.1770 `loudness`, true-peak `limit`/`master`, `analyze`, `spectrogram_png`) and `TimeRefs` (timeline references) |
| `audio/sfx.py` | SFX synthesizer (`--list`, `--spectro DIR`, or build some by name) |

## The score (`audio/music.py`)

### Themes (all original)
- **JESUS, "the reach"**, in F major: 1-6-5-3, a rising sixth that opens like arms (F4 D5 C5 A4). It is
  answered by 2-3-5-2 (a half cadence) or 4-3-2-1 (home). It has a 2-note cell (F4→D5, the sixth alone)
  and a cadence tail.
- **THE VOID**, in D minor: 1-5-b6-5 (D A Bb A), a vast slow breath whose b6 sighs. It becomes **tender**
  as 1-5-6-5 (F C D C). That 6-5 sigh is the same one that ends Jesus's reach, so the two themes fit
  together and walk off in counterpoint at the end of Part One (celesta theme over the cello). Other forms:
  **sad** (borrowed b6 over Bbm6, for the sad smile), **bloom** (D A B A → F#, opening into the galaxies),
  and as a **waddle** (the bassoon and low pizzicato plodding root-5-b6-5 behind the fleeing user).
- **FEAR / anxiety**: a skittering neighbour-note flicker (3-2-3-2-3 . 5) for the user's panic. In Part
  Two it becomes Fear's own hopeful 3-2-3-5 cell, a callback to the creator's earlier film "The Void and My
  Fear". It turns into a question on solo flute ("What's that noise?") and blooms lydian among the
  galaxies.
- Smaller figures: the sly clarinet line (D dorian), the magician's chromatic slither (low clarinet and
  bassoon), the exit grumble, comic pizzicato "plink", bassoon "bwomp" and the "shrug".

**Keys.** Part One is in F major, with the Void's D minor as its shadow (the same notes around a darker
centre). The bonus scene shifts to D at the dissolve, a chromatic-mediant move into the "other world":
creepy D minor first, then D major for the cosmos. Tonal SFX use only D, E and A, so they sound right in
both keys.

### Moods (textures)
Each plan cue has one mood. Every texture thins out under speech (pads, soft pulses, high pings); melodies
go into the gaps. Lyrical moods only need a phrase's first three notes to fit in a gap; the rest of the
phrase can continue softly under the next line. Comic moods keep whole phrases inside the gaps, so the
comic timing stays clean.

**Level.** Each cue is calibrated to its mood's loudness target, plus 5 dB per unit of intensity above
0.4 (clamped to ±2.5 dB). Hand-edited moods therefore keep a consistent dramatic arc: sparse ≈ -27,
comic-light ≈ -22, lyrical ≈ -20, chase/romp ≈ -17, cosmos peak ≈ -15.5 LUFS. The arc is measured before
the whole score is normalised to -18. Ramps and swells inside a cue are kept.

**Transitions.** Cues change at scene boundaries (or inside a scene where a two-part mood text gets
split). The old chord releases into the new one: the old cue fades over about 1.4 s, the new one enters
over 0.5 s, and some moods start with a pickup gesture (a harp glissando). Comic boundaries are a
**cut**, chosen when the music text says "record-scratch / stop / near-silence" or "stops dead". The
Part One → Part Two **dissolve** uses a long crossfade. Every cadence-ending cue clears out before the
next world begins.

### The plan (`data/music_plan.json`)
On the first run the plan is derived from each scene's `music` text (mood, instrumentation and intensity),
plus its slug and purpose. Keywords are scored (mood text ×3, instrumentation ×1.5, the rest ×1) and the
result is mapped to the moods allowed at the scene's location. Two-part texts such as "X, then Y" split
the scene at the first beat that mentions the second mood (for example the beat where the magician is
mentioned, or the kick). Neighbouring scenes with the same mood merge into one continuous cue. The last
Part One cue becomes `walkoff` with an ending, and the last cue of the film becomes `cosmos` with an
ending. A text ending in "dead stop" or "cuts out" adds a stop-time over a trailing silent beat.

On later runs the plan is **loaded as-is**, so hand edits stick. If the screenplay's scene list (ids, slugs,
locations) or a scene's `music` notes change, or a plan reference no longer exists, the plan is derived again
and the old one is kept as `data/music_plan.json.bak`. To force a fresh plan,
run `python3 audio/music.py --replan`. To print the plan without rendering, use `--plan-only`. To see
every note, write a piano roll with `--roll out/music_roll.png`.

```json
{"music_lufs": -18,
 "accents": {"enabled": true, "gain_db": 0, "min_spacing": 0.9, "skip": ["s04b06:teehee"], "high_instr": null},
 "auto_hits": {"enabled": true, "kick_thump": "stop", "block_thunk": "wahwah"},
 "cues": [{"id": "c07", "scenes": ["s07"], "from": "s07", "to": "s07.end", "mood": "tender", "key": "F", "bpm": 66,
           "intensity": 0.25, "ramp": [[0, 0.9], [1, 1.1]], "transition_in": "crossfade", "ending": false,
           "melody_stem": "cello", "layers_off": ["harp"], "gain_db": 0, "ending_hold": 6,
           "prog": [["F", 4], ["Dm7", 4]], "hits": [{"t": "s07b04+0.2", "kind": "swell", "len": 4}]}]}
```
Cue fields:
- `from` / `to`: timeline references.
- `key`: the tonic. The mood's mode is kept, and the transposition is folded to within ±6 semitones.
- `intensity`: 0..1. It sets the layers, velocities and level.
- `ramp`: multipliers on intensity across the cue.
- `transition_in`: `crossfade`, `cut` or `dissolve`.
- `ending`: a cadence after the last line, then a held chord ringing for `ending_hold` seconds.
- `layers_off`: instrument names to drop.
- `gain_db`: a level trim.
- `prog`: chords in the mood's reference key (F for major Part One moods, Dm for minor ones, D for Part
  Two major).

### Word-painting accents (`^word` high, `~word` low)
After TTS, `data/timeline.json` gives each mark an absolute time (the Whisper word start). Before TTS, the
time is estimated from the word's position in the line. For each mark:
- **high**: a chord tone of the current harmony, in key, around D6–A6 (the targets rotate for variety).
  It is played on **glockenspiel + celesta** in comic cues, **harp + celesta** in tender cues and
  **celesta + glockenspiel** elsewhere, with a softer glint a 4th–6th above, 85 ms later.
- **low**: the current bass or root on **contrabass** (bowed, pre-rolled 70 ms so its slow attack peaks on
  the word), with **low piano** in octaves, a soft **timpani** stroke and a cello.

Measured on the test mix: attacks land on the word onset within ±15 ms (bowed low notes within +30 ms).
In the final mix, the accents sit about 8 dB under the word they paint (range -12 to -2 dB). Accents
closer than 0.9 s to the previous one are skipped, as are accents inside a stop-time; `data/music_report.json`
lists each accent and why one was skipped. To silence one, add `"beat:word"` to `accents.skip`. To change
all of them, set `accents.gain_db`. They are rendered into `out/stems/music_features.wav`, which the mix
ducks only 2 dB under speech, while the bed ducks 7.5 dB, so they are felt on the words.

### Comic hits
Animators add hits in their scene cue file, `data/cues/<sceneId>.json` → `"hits": [...]`, or in
`data/music_hits.json` (a list), or in a plan cue's `hits`. Each hit is `{"t": ref|seconds, "kind": ...,
"len": s}`:

| kind | effect |
|---|---|
| `sting` | a quick accent on the current chord: pizzicato + low pizzicato + timpani + a glockenspiel glint |
| `stop` | **stop-time**: the score (dry and reverb) drops to silence in 30 ms and comes back over 0.35 s after `len` (default 2.5 s). With `"stab": true` (the default for stops) an orchestral thump (bass drum + timpani + pizzicato) lands exactly on `t` |
| `swell` | a crescendo layer (strings, plus choir in lyrical moods) blooming over `len` seconds (default 4) from `t`; the cue's intensity rises too |
| `wahwah` | muted trumpet "wah-wah-wah-waaah", descending in semitones from the 5th, with a plunger-style brightness envelope and a drooping wobble on the last note; soft, tasteful |
| `tada` | a harp/glockenspiel run up into a horn chord + timpani + cymbal on `t` |
| `creep` | a low clarinet/bassoon chromatic slither + a bowed-glass tritone |

Automatic hits, which explicit ones override when they fall within 1 s:
- `kick_thump` → `stop`, lasting through the flight and crash plus 1.3 s of silence.
- `block_thunk` → `wahwah`, 0.25 s after the thunk.

The slapstick cue also answers each stop with a xylophone tumble and a triangle.

## Sound effects (`audio/sfx.py`, `tools/collect_sfx.py`)

with `gain_db: 0` already sits correctly against dialogue at about -18 LUFS. Every file is DC-free and
faded, with true peak ≤ -1 dBFS and no clicks.

**Sync.** `sync` is where the hit sits inside the file. An event's `t` is the moment the sound **lands**,
and the mixer places the file at `t − sync`. **Variants** (`name_2.wav`, …) rotate automatically.

### Automatic placement (from each beat's `sfx` list)
- **Footsteps** loop through the beat; consecutive beats join into one walk. In a line, they start on a
  matching verb ("storms" → stomps).
- **`wind_soft`** becomes a bed over the beat.
- **One-shots** land on a matching word of the line, using Whisper times once TTS exists. Examples:
  `kick_thump` on "kicks", `block_thunk` on "blocks" (with `phone_tap` 0.35 s before it), `pat_pat` on
  "pats", `dagger_shing` on "dagger", `lock_click` and `cage_creak` on "opened". Otherwise a one-shot plays
  near the beat start, and several in one beat are staggered by 0.4 s.
- **`bleep`** is dropped when `tts.py` already put the bleep into the line audio.
- Unknown names print a warning. `star_cough` is an alias of `star_pop`.

### Precise cues from scene animators (override the automatic ones)
Each scene's animator writes their own file, so parallel writers never collide:
```json
// data/cues/s13.json
{"sfx":  [{"t": "s13b08+0.42", "name": "kick_thump", "gain_db": 0, "pan": 0.2},
          {"t": "s13b12+0.5", "name": "footsteps_soft", "until": "s13b12.end", "gain_db": -3}],
 "hits": [{"t": "s13b08+0.42", "kind": "stop", "len": 3.0}]}
```
- `t` is seconds or a **timeline reference**: `s13b08`, `s13b08+0.42`, `s05b02.end-0.3`, `s05b02.mid`,
  `s08` (scene start), `s08.start+1`, `s08.end`, `film.end`.
- A cue **replaces the automatic events of the same name in the same beat**: the beat named by the
  reference, or the beat that contains its time.
- Optional fields:
  - `until`: loopable sounds loop until then; other sounds are cut there with a fade.
  - `fade`: the fade length.
  - `variant`: 1..n, to pick a specific file.
  - `rate`: varispeed.
- The older single file `data/sfx_cues.json` (a list, or `{"sfx": [...]}`) still works.
- Unreadable or half-written files are skipped with a message.
- For tests, the environment variable `CUES_DIR` points at another cues folder and `LINES_JSON` at
  another `lines.json`.

## The mix (`audio/mix.py`)

**Voices.** Each clip is first levelled to a per-speaker loudness, within -8..+12 dB, so deliveries keep
their shape. Then each group gets its own processing:
- **NARRATOR**: dry-warm and centred. Low-cut at 75 Hz, +1 dB warmth at 180 Hz, +1.5 dB presence at
  3 kHz, gentle 2.2:1 compression, a hint of small room.
- **JESUS / USER** (outdoors at dusk): a light, short, airy room (0.55 s, 10 % wet). Jesus is panned
  slightly left, the user slightly right.
- **FEAR / back-room dialogue**: the back room's warmer, longer reverb (1.2 s).
- **MAGICIAN**: slightly creepy. A slow detuned double (chorus), +1.5 dB low-shelf, darker highs, a
  longer and darker room (1.8 s, 20 %), placed a touch right and behind.
- **VOID vocals** (laughs, huffs, giggles, sighs…): huge and cosmic. A subtle octave-down layer (rubberband,
  -9 dB, low-passed), wide, and a long "vast" reverb (4.5 s) whose feed includes a faint octave-up
  **shimmer**.
- **Whispers** (any speaker): close and intimate. Dry, with a little air and centred; the Void's whispers
  keep a faint cosmic tail.

**Score.** The bed is EQ'd to leave the voice's low end free (high-pass 40 Hz, -2 dB below 120 Hz). It
sits 9 dB under its file level and is **side-chain ducked 7.5 dB** under all speech: 0.15 s look-ahead
attack, a 0.55 s hold that bridges the breaths between phrases, 0.9 s release. The features stem
(accents and hits) sits at -14 dB and ducks only 2 dB.

**SFX and ambience.** SFX get a touch of the location's room and dip 4 dB under speech, so the dialogue
stays on top. The ambience beds play per location: crossfaded at location changes, with a 2.5 s
crossfade at the dissolve.

**Master.** The master gain is estimated first. The dialogue and SFX buses are then peak-limited about
1 dB under the ceiling, so the TTS crest factor doesn't drive the master. The sum is loudness-normalised
to **-14 LUFS** and true-peak limited to **-1.5 dBTP** (4× oversampled look-ahead limiter), leaving
headroom for AAC. Options: `--lufs`, `--tp`, `--no-music`, `--no-sfx`, `--music-dir`.

**Measured** on a full test with real TTS (faithful draft, 4:48):
- Integrated -14.0 LUFS, true peak -1.50 dBTP, click score 5.8 (no clicks), DC 0.
- Dialogue during speech: -12 to -14.5 LUFS in every scene.
- Score bed: about 15 dB under the voice while someone speaks, and 6–9 dB under it in the pauses.
- Ambience: about -35 LUFS.
- The master limiter is active in only 1.2 % of 10 ms blocks.

## Gotchas
- Before TTS (estimated timeline) the mix has no voices and accent times are estimates. **After TTS, run
  `tools/build_timeline.py` and then `tools/build_audio.sh`.**
- The plan persists across re-timings (TTS, pacing). It is re-derived (old one kept as `.bak`) when the scene
  list or a scene's `music` notes change, or when one of its references no longer exists — copy your hand
  edits back from the `.bak` if needed.
- Melodies need gaps. Scenes with wall-to-wall narration get textures and pings only, plus phrase heads
  where a breath allows. That is intended, so the voice stays on top.
- Do not add reverb to SFX in scene code: the files and the mix already carry their rooms.
