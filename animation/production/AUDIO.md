# Audio sources and mix notes

## Dialogue: offline neural text-to-speech (Piper 1.8.0)

No human voice actors were used. Every line is generated with the [Piper](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/piper1-gpl) text-to-speech engine, using voices from [rhasspy/piper-voices](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices). I checked the license of each voice's training data in its `MODEL_CARD`.

None of these is safe for public posting. They were swapped for the voices above. The original cast table is kept in git history (commit "Add 'A Table Instead of a Palace' …") if you want it for private use.

**Processing:**
- Each sentence is generated separately, with silence trimmed.
- Pitch changes are done by changing playback rate, combined with a matching length scale so the speaking pace stays the same.
- Rooms are simulated with convolution reverb using a decaying-noise impulse response:
  - kitchen "room": 0.35 s decay
  - halls: 1.3 s decay
  - the gate chamber where David weeps: 2.2 s decay
  - voice-over: dry, no reverb
- The synthesis noise in the TTS model isn't seeded, so the generated audio is cached as `cache/*.npy`, keyed by voice, speaker, text and pacing. Rebuilds are identical as long as the cache is kept.

**Speaking rates (characters per second):**
- Teo 15, Noa 15, Solomon 17, Jesus 19, David 18.
- Peter is deliberately fast at about 25 because he's panicking.
- Solomon, Jesus, Peter, Abigail, young Solomon and old David were slowed after a first measurement showed they were rushing their lines.

## Music and sound effects

All music and effects are generated by `source/music.py` with numpy and scipy. No recordings, samples or instrument libraries were used.

**Instruments (all synthesized):**
- Plucked lyre (Karplus–Strong string model).
- Additive-synthesis bells.
- Bowed-string "cello" and brass-like swells.
- Simple piano model.
- Detuned-saw pads.
- Frame drum, tambourine, wind, fire crackle and door knock, all built from synthesized noise and tone.

**Score plan:**
- Each scene has a mood with its own key, chord progression and instrument set (the `MOODS` table).
- A four-note lyre motif belongs to David. It returns under the Jesus scenes without the war drums: "David's heart, without the sword".

**Accents on words:**
- A high bell sounds on each word marked `^` (hopeful).
- A deep cello note sounds on each word marked `~` (heavy).
- Timing within a sentence is estimated by character position, which is approximate to about ±0.2 s.

**Sound effects, each tied to the frame of its visible action** (frame numbers are listed in `SHOT_LIST.md`):
- Peter's sword draw.
- The sword landing in the grass.
- Uriah's helmet landing in the dust.
- The knock on the door. Both characters react on the knock frame.
- The festival crowd bed.
- The ending chord swell.

**Quiet moments:** the music thins out for the weeping scenes, the Ecclesiastes hall (mostly wind and a low cello) and the "zero doors" cutaway.

## Mix

- **Ducking:** the music drops by up to 45% when someone speaks, with a fast attack and slow release, measured from the dialogue loudness.
- **Spread:** music and effects are spread across the stereo field, while dialogue is centered.
- **Loudness target:** −14 LUFS integrated, −1.0 dBTP true peak, which is a typical target for social and mobile platforms.
- **Processing chain:** loudness normalization in two passes (measure, then apply linearly), followed by a 4× oversampled peak limiter set to −3.1 dBFS.
- **Measured on the final MP4:** see `EXPORT.md`.
- **Level balance:** in the first mix pass, stretches of music between lines measured about 5 dB below speech sections.

**Listening review:** none. I can't play audio in this environment, so the mix has not been checked by ear. Everything above was verified by measurement only. Please listen for:
- voices that sound robotic or mispronounced
- music that is too loud under quiet lines
- the women's chorus, which is a layered blend of six voices
