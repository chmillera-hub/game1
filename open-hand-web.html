The Open Hand

A web of non-violent, pro-human responses to the usual reasons given for force and cages

The rule of this world
  1. No one may touch another person without consent. Not police, not military, not citizens.
  2. No one may be caged. Not "criminals", not protesters, not patients.
  3. The rule is symmetric. If an officer breaks it, nobody may cage or grab the officer either. If a citizen breaks it, the same.
  4. So the penalty for breaking the rule can never be the thing the rule forbids. Every response has to be non-violent and consensual.

It's a closed loop: the rule binds the citizen, the officer, and whoever responds to the officer. No layer gets a free pass to do what it claims to be stopping, so a "secondary predator" (someone looking for a righteous excuse to hurt people) has nothing to feed on.

What you can do

Keep your distance from the fists. Name the behavior out loud. Witness. Talk. Ask what's needed and offer it. Stand between people without touching anyone. Invite people to move away from danger. Offer comfort, but only touch after asking (a hug, a hand on the shoulder). Process it through art, circles, mentorship and spiritual growth. Withdraw trust and roles. Wait. Keep coming back.

How to play

Each scenario opens with one of the common justifications for force. Pick a response. Force gets called out and taken apart using the world's own logic, and you try again. Non-violent responses hold and move the story forward. Each one you use lights up a strand in The Web. Every scenario ends with its hardest objection, stated honestly, and how this world answers it.

When someone asks "but what if…?", find it here. Each answer replaces force instead of excusing it, and is honest about what it costs.
