# What If No One Could Use Force?

A portrait (1080×1920, 24 fps) animated short, 5:15. It is 2 AM in a 24-hour diner, the night before the
No-Force Law takes effect. The law says nobody (police, soldiers, or citizens) may touch another person
without consent or lock anyone in a cage. Breaking the rule is never answered with force or cages either,
only with words, distance, calling it out, and repair. Officer Dana Hale sits alone with her duty belt on
the table. Kai, the night cook, pours her a refill, asks before he sits down, and sketches the new world in
his notebook while a narrator fills in the bigger picture. Halfway through, Kai tells her where they met
before.

## What the story explores

* **The toolkit without force:** words, witnesses, your own body placed between people (if you choose),
  reaching for the person being hurt *if they reach back*, a whole room yelling "stop".
* **The honest cost:** some harm that force could have stopped will happen anyway. Force also has its own
  victims. This world bets the trade is worth it.
* **The cells open:** more than 11 million people are in prison worldwide. Many are unconvicted, sick,
  addicted, or poor, and some did terrible things. They still exist. The rule only changes what everyone
  else may do about them.
* **Distance, not cages:** a community can step back from someone who keeps hurting people. That is a
  boundary with the door left open, followed by a restorative circle when they are ready.
* **The new job:** most police calls aren't violent. Here they go to people trained to talk, treat and stay,
  and the money once spent on cages funds prevention.
* **Soldiers and tanks:** the only defence left is refusal, through strikes, empty streets and no one
  obeying.
* **Calling it out:** Kai names what Hale did to him at fifteen. She listens and apologises. He doesn't
  have to forgive her. At dawn she asks to shake his hand, and he offers a fist bump instead: every touch
  is a question.

Facts the narrator states:

* World prison population above 11 million: *World Prison Population List*, ICPR / World Prison Brief.
* Restorative-justice conferences: victims are more satisfied and less vengeful than after court
  (Strang et al., Campbell Systematic Review 2013).
* Nonviolent campaigns 1900–2006 succeeded about twice as often as violent ones, 53 % vs 26 %
  (Stephan & Chenoweth, *International Security* 2008).
* Only a small share of police calls involve violence (e.g. Vera Institute's 2022 analysis of New
  Orleans 911 calls found about 5 %).

## How it is made

Everything is generated by code in `src/`:

- `script.py`: screenplay (lines, pauses, music accents on hopeful/heavy words)
- `tts.py`: voices with Kokoro-82M (Hale `af_heart` −2 st, Kai `am_fenrir`, narrator `bm_fable`,
  TV anchor `am_michael`), word timestamps + phonemes, checked with Whisper for intelligibility
- `timeline.py`, `anchors.py`, `direction.py`: absolute timeline, shot list, music map, SFX cues
- `rig.py`, `poses.py`, `cast.py`, `acting.py`, `acting_script.py`: Skia character rig (pseudo-3D head
  turns, lids/brows/iris gaze, wet eyes and tears, phoneme lip-sync, blinks, micro-saccades, breathing,
  gestures such as the flinch, unvoiced chatter for the time-lapse) and the per-word performance script
- `scene_diner.py`, `shots_diner.py`: the diner set (rainy window, wall TV, booth, checkered floor),
  camera moves, the night-to-dawn light, the coffee pour and the fist bump
- `scene_sketch.py`, `sketchbook.py`: Kai's hand-drawn notebook pages for the narration
- `music.py`, `sfx.py`, `audio_dsp.py`, `audio_mix.py`: procedural score, sound effects, ducking, loudness
- `subtitles.py`: phrase cards with the spoken word highlighted in the speaker's colour, plus SRT
- `render.py`, `encode.py`: parallel frame rendering and the size-capped two-pass encode

Rebuild (Python 3.12 venv with `kokoro`, `skia-python`, `numpy`, `scipy`, `soundfile`, `pyloudnorm`,
`pillow`; `espeak-ng` and `ffmpeg` on PATH):

```bash
cd src
python fetch_fonts.py    # handwriting fonts (OFL) -> build/fonts
python tts.py            # voices  -> build/voice
python timeline.py       # timeline -> build/timeline.json
python audio_mix.py      # score + sfx + mix -> build/audio/mix.wav
python subtitles.py      # SRT -> output/
python encode.py all     # render + encode -> output/
```

Intermediate files go to `$WHATIF_BUILD` (see `paths.py`).
