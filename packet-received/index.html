Packet Received

You are the packet: one person’s need for deeper connection, community and room to grow, plus the conditions behind it. Each month society hands you routes. Your goal is to be received by as many of the town’s 1,000 people as you can, and every card shows the exact math of who would receive you.

    How the lens works

    Every route has a number of people who hear you directly, k, and a fidelity, φ: the share of your 8 fields that survives the channel, times the share still intact in your payload. Each person who hears you passes you on to R = R₀ · φ others on average, so a distorted message travels less.

    E = k · (1 + R + R²)   contacts over two more generations

    Contacts land on people at random, and some of them already have the packet. If r of the town’s N = 1,000 people have it, the expected number of new people is

    new = (N − r) · (1 − e−E/N)

    The outcome is drawn around that expectation (±25%), and risky routes roll their risk. Some of the new people become carriers, who are the base for compare notes, mutual aid and organizing. Every month costs 2 energy (work or perish). Once 20 carriers have your back, it costs 1.

    The four tests from Packet Loss sit on every card: OUT × INTACT × SIDEWAYS × UPWARD. A route can change the conditions only when all four are 1. Reach is the score. Passing the four tests decides whether the year ends with the conditions in play.

    The routes reward the same thing the world does. Standard advice restores energy and keeps you alive, which is real, but on average it reaches about 5 people a year. Across 3,000 simulated years, random choices averaged about 210 people and careful play about 750.
