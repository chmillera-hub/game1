# Anatomy and visual lessons: the creator's checklist

Extracted from the creator's notes on another animation (the Paul and Asher film). Only the body, anatomy and visual lessons are kept. Every dialogue, voice, sound, music and story note is left out, and the timestamps there don't apply here. Each lesson is a check to run across the whole of Tiredness Episode 2. Where it doesn't apply, ignore it.

## Hands and held objects
- Hands must look anatomically correct, never a blob. No outlines inside the hand that break up its structure, and "no little blobs of ovals": inner shapes should blend into the surrounding colour.
- An object held in the hand must not show the thumb through it. The grip shows either the fingers or the thumb, the way it's really held.
- An object held in the palm sits in the palm, with the fingers wrapped around it anatomically.
- A character reading a letter visibly holds the paper or scroll, e.g. by both sides.
- Hands rest on the legs (palm down) when seated, "instead of dangling in the air".
- Palms face the right way, e.g. toward the face when the gesture calls for it, not toward the camera.

## Arms, elbows and shoulders
- Arms are attached to the body. Elbows look natural and anatomically correct, on both arms.
- Arms don't clip into, or "teleport through", clothing. An arm comes out from under a draped garment properly, and a garment crossing the body sits on the shoulder.
- Shoulder shadows blend into the clothing. No hard brown line that "looks like a piece of mud", and no mud-like blob by the shoulder.

## Neck and head
- No stray line on the neck that isn't part of the anatomy.
- The neck is a plausible length, not too long.
- The neck is angled the way the head is looking, not zig-zagging "like a Z".
- The head sits correctly on the neck, not too high or too far to one side.
- Add subtle neck contours, the V-shaped neck muscles, where appropriate.
- No "floating head": the neck or the top of the clothing shows.
- Head coverings sit down on the scalp, not floating high, and have some detail.
- The top of the head is blended, not like "two pieces of mud slapped on top".

## Face, eyes and hair
- Cheeks are smooth and anatomically average, with no sharp inward notch.
- Eyes look at the person being addressed, not at the ground.
- Gaze changes are smooth: no rapid jump from looking down to looking aside. One natural look, not a double look.
- Hair is blended and natural, with no "little circle things".
- Side strands lie against the head, not floating in the air over the ears.

## Tears
- A teardrop is one object that slowly fades out, not a drop that "disappears in parts".
- Tears fade out naturally after falling, for every character.
- Tears are a little blue, not white "like his beard".
- Tears start at the right moment in a beat, not right at the beginning.

## Body motion
- A tiny bit of body sway, not "standing almost completely stiff like a statue".
- No body shakes: no "glitches up and down rapidly for like 0.1 seconds".
- No head "teleports", and no jitter: head rotation is smooth.
- Movement is "smoother and more anatomically timed".
- In a shot, every character's body, arms and head are animated, so they look lifelike. That includes figures in the background and the distance.

## Legs, feet and sitting
- Characters have proper legs.
- Seated poses are physically possible. Not both legs over one side while the body faces the other way; one leg down and the other resting naturally.
- Feet point the way the body faces, e.g. toward the viewer when the character faces the screen.
- Legs are defined under long clothing, not one solid blob.
- A kneeling pose reads as kneeling, with the clothing over the knees.

## Light, props and colour
- A character standing near a light isn't unnaturally dark. Shadows and light on people look natural.
- Props rest on surfaces (e.g. a small table) instead of floating in the air.
- Clothing colour stays consistent, with no unexplained dark recolouring.
