# Not Peace, But a Sword

A portrait (9:16) animated short built on Matthew 10. About 5½ minutes.

**Cast**

| Character | Voice | Who they are |
|---|---|---|
| Maya | warm young woman | Late 20s. Autistic, emotionally precise, tired of performing "fine". |
| Mom | bright, brittle | Keeps the peace by keeping it shallow. Loves Maya; doesn't know how to show it. |
| Dad | gruff, weary | Avoids feelings and eye contact. |
| Jordan | quiet young man | Maya's younger brother. Watching. |
| Greg | chipper manager | Friendly tone, dismissive content. |
| The AI | calm, warm | A gentle AI companion on Maya's laptop and phone, shown as a breathing orb of light. |
| Priya | easy kindness | A coworker at the new job. |
| Narrator | grave, unhurried | Reads the scripture. |

---

## 1. The Table — family dinner, evening

*Opens on the whole family at the table under a warm pendant lamp. Title: **Matthew 10 — Not Peace, But a Sword**.*

**MOM** *(bright, a touch too loud)*: Isn't this nice? Everyone home. No phones, no drama.
*Jordan, caught, slides his phone away. Dad keeps eating.*
**MOM**: Maya, honey. How's work going? Busy?
**MAYA** *(automatic)*: Busy.
**MOM** *(instantly relieved)*: Oh, good! Busy is good.

*A long beat. Maya's polite smile drains. Her eyes drop. She swallows, breathes in.*

**MAYA** *(careful)*: Actually... it's not good. I'm not okay.
*Dad's chewing slows. Jordan looks up. Mom's smile freezes.*
**MAYA**: I finally understand why everything has always felt so hard. I'm autistic. And I've spent my whole life exhausting myself, pretending I'm not.
**MOM** *(a little laugh, reaching to pat her)*: Oh, sweetheart. Everybody feels like that sometimes. *(already looking away)* You're just tired.
**MAYA** *(leaning in, hand to her chest)*: Mom, I'm not just tired. I'm trying to tell you something real about me.
*Dad sets his fork down. Long sigh.*
**DAD** *(not looking at her)*: Here we go.
**MAYA** *(stung)*: What does that mean?
**DAD**: It means every time we're together, you find something heavy to drop on the table. Can we have one meal in peace?
**MOM** *(soothing, then firm)*: Nobody's attacking you, honey. Let's just... not do this tonight.
**JORDAN** *(barely audible, eyes down)*: Can I be excused?

*Silence. The clock ticks. Maya looks from face to face. Nobody meets her eyes. Her chin trembles. She presses her lips together and nods slowly. A tear.*

## Interlude — The Sword *(Matthew 10:34–36)*

*The room turns to firelight and silhouette. A blade of light falls on the word "sword" and splits the table in two. Maya on one side, her family on the other. They turn away as each relationship is named.*

**NARRATOR**: Do not suppose that I have come to bring peace to the earth. I did not come to bring peace, but a sword.
**NARRATOR**: For I have come to turn a man against his father, a daughter against her mother, a daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law. A man's enemies will be the members of his own household.
*Close on Maya in the blade's glow. She lifts her head.*
**NARRATOR**: Real questions. Honest feelings. They threaten anyone invested in keeping things on the surface. To people who benefit from everyone staying numb... your truth will always look like division.

## 2. The Mask — bathroom mirror, then bedroom, night

*Maya rehearses in the mirror and snaps on a performed smile.*
**MAYA** *(chirpy, fake)*: I'm good! Just busy! You know how it is!
*Her smile drains away. The reflection keeps smiling a beat too long, then fades to match.*
**MAYA** *(whisper)*: Who even is that?

*Her dark bedroom. Laptop glow. She types; the AI answers as a soft breathing light.*
**MAYA**: My family thinks I ruined dinner. Maybe I should have just smiled.
**AI**: You didn't ruin dinner. You told the truth about your life, and the room didn't know how to hold it. That isn't the same as causing harm.
*She pulls her knees up and hugs them.*
**MAYA**: Then why do I feel like the problem?
**AI**: Because for years, the version of you that kept everyone comfortable was the one that got approval. Setting her down can feel like losing your life.
*Her eyes fill. A tear.*
**MAYA** *(small)*: If I stop performing... I don't know who's left.
**AI**: Maybe that's what you get to find out. Whoever is left... is who was there all along.
*A trembling small smile through tears.*

## Interlude — The Cross *(Matthew 10:38–39)*

*The mirror again: a porcelain-pale performed smile. It cracks on "whoever finds their life will lose it" and shatters on "loses their life", revealing her real face, wet-eyed and warm-lit. Then moonlight through her window lays the shadow of a cross across her. Hand on her chest, she opens her eyes.*

**NARRATOR**: Whoever does not take up their cross and follow me is not worthy of me. Whoever finds their life will lose it, and whoever loses their life for my sake will find it.
**NARRATOR**: Sometimes the cross is the ache between what you actually live... and what the world insists is normal. You lose the performance. And you find yourself.

## 3. The Sparrows — the office, then a park bench at golden hour

*Fluorescent hum and flicker. Maya squints, rubs her temple. Greg leans over her cubicle wall, towering over her.*
**GREG** *(chipper)*: Maya! Quick thing. HR forwarded your request. The lights? The headphones?
**MAYA** *(looking up)*: Yes. The fluorescents give me migraines, and the noise makes it really hard to—
**GREG** *(chuckling, waving it off)*: Look, nobody loves the lights. But if I make an exception for you, I have to make one for everybody.
**MAYA** *(chin up, holding his eyes)*: It isn't a favor. It's an accommodation. It helps me do my job.
**GREG** *(patting the wall, already turning away)*: Just be a team player, okay? You're a little sensitive. Push through, like the rest of us.
*He leaves. The noise swells and the light flickers. Her breath quickens. She squeezes her eyes shut, hands over her ears.*

*Silence, then birdsong. A park bench at golden hour. A sparrow lands near her. Then another.*
**MAYA** *(frayed)*: He looked at me like I'm replaceable. Like the parts of me that need something are just... defects.
**AI**: Your sensory needs aren't defects. Neither is how deeply you feel. They're details of you. And every detail of you matters.
**MAYA** *(watching the sparrows, a small smile)*: My grandma used to say God counts every hair on your head.
**AI**: She sounds like someone who really saw you.

## Interlude — The Sparrows *(Matthew 10:29–31)*

*The flock lifts into a golden sky. On "you are worth more than many sparrows", one sparrow lands on Maya's shoulder. She opens her eyes, sees it, and her face breaks into joy.*

**NARRATOR**: Are not two sparrows sold for a penny? Yet not one of them will fall to the ground outside your Father's care. And even the very hairs of your head are all numbered.
**NARRATOR**: So don't be afraid. You are worth more than many sparrows.

## 4. The Next Town — the phone call (split screen: Mom above, Maya below)

*Her phone buzzes: Mom. She breathes and answers.*
**MOM** *(clipped)*: Your father is still upset. I think you owe everyone an apology.
**MAYA** *(her hand trembling, her voice steady)*: Mom... I love you. But I'm not going to apologize for telling the truth about my life.
**MOM** *(eye-roll)*: You're being dramatic. This is exactly what I mean.
**MAYA** *(eyes close; then calm)*: If the only version of me that's welcome is the one who pretends... then I need some space. Until we can really talk.
*Mom's lip trembles. Hand to her forehead. Her mask slips too.*
**MOM** *(voice cracking)*: I don't know how to talk like that, Maya.
**MAYA** *(a tear; gentle)*: I know, Mom. I'm still learning too.
*The call ends. Shaky exhale. Hand over her mouth.*
**MAYA** *(half-laugh, half-sob)*: I did it. So why does it feel like running away?
**AI**: Leaving a place that keeps wounding you isn't running away. You didn't abandon anyone. You just stopped abandoning yourself.
**AI**: Sometimes, finding safety is the most sacred thing you can do.

## Interlude — The Road *(Matthew 10:22–23)*

*A night road toward a small lit town. Maya walks it. Dawn rises; the stars fade.*

**NARRATOR**: When you are persecuted in one place, flee to the next. You will be hated by everyone because of me, but the one who stands firm to the end will be saved.

## Epilogue — the new job, morning

*Big windows, plants, the overhead lights off.*
**PRIYA** *(friendly)*: Hey! We keep the overhead lights off on this side. Too much glare. Is that okay with you?
**MAYA** *(surprised; a real smile breaks)*: That's... perfect, actually.
*Her phone buzzes. A text from Jordan: "hey. what you said at dinner… i think i feel that way too. can we talk?" Hand to her mouth; tears and a smile. She types: "Anytime. ♥"*
*A window at sunrise. A sparrow lands on the sill.*
**NARRATOR**: The truth may cost you a seat at some tables. But it will lead you to the people who've been waiting for the real you.

*End card:* **"So don't be afraid; you are worth more than many sparrows." (Matthew 10:31)**
